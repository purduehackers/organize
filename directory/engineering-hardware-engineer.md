# [Engineering] Hardware Engineer

Hack Night should feel a little bit like walking into a secret lab. We have a glowing meter-tall sign. A custom NFC-based system to open doors after-hours. Phone bells that ring at Hack Night. Tons of strange sensors, buttons, lights, kiosks, machines, and physical objects that make the room feel more alive.

We’re looking for Hardware Engineers to help build that physical side of Purdue Hackers!

This role is for people who want to make things that exist in the real world: electronics, embedded systems, interactive installations, props, tools, infrastructure, and weird little machines that make Hack Night feel more magical. Some projects will be practical. Some will be completely unnecessary. The best ones are usually both.

You might work a physical check-in system, room displays, a gong that rings when people ship something, interactive installations for events, or something nobody has thought of yet. The point is to create artifacts that make Purdue Hackers feel like more than a club. We want the space itself to feel hackable!

You do not need to be a hardware expert already. A lot of our best hardware projects have come from people learning as they go, debugging cursed electronics at Hack Night, asking friends for help, and slowly turning a wacky idea into something real. The Sign, for example, took a year and a half, around ten people, custom PCBs, CAD, 3D printing, LEDs, firmware, budgeting mistakes, networking pain, and multiple controller revisions before it finally shipped.

## What you will do

- Design and build hardware projects, installations, and physical infrastructure for Purdue Hackers
- Work on electronics, embedded software, sensors, lights, buttons, displays, enclosures, PCBs, 3D-printed parts, and other physical systems
- Help create interactive objects for Hack Night, The Show, workshops, callouts, and other events
- Maintain and improve existing hardware projects like the Sign, phone bells, door systems, displays, and other room artifacts
- Collaborate with designers, engineers, and organizers to turn strange ideas into real objects
- Prototype quickly, test at Hack Night, break things, fix them, and make the next version better
- Document your projects so future Hackers can understand, repair, remix, and build on them

## About you

You will be a great fit for this role if you:

- Like building physical things, even when they are messy, cursed, or held together by hope
- Are interested in electronics, embedded systems, CAD, fabrication, firmware, robotics, or interactive installations
- Enjoy turning vague ideas like “what if the room had a giant glowing thing?” into actual working prototypes
- Are willing to learn unfamiliar tools, debug weird problems, and ask other people for help
- Care about making things that people can touch, use, notice, and remember
- Are excited by the idea of building artifacts that become part of Purdue Hackers culture

Bonus points if you:

- Have experience with Arduino, ESP32, Raspberry Pi, PCB design, soldering, CAD, 3D printing, laser cutting, firmware, robotics, or electronics
- Are technical or willing to learn
- Are a natural leader & are willing to eventually lead a small team
- Are weird and/or have felt in the past like you struggled to find your people

Many of these are just suggestions; please apply if you are interested even if you don't feel like you tick every box.
